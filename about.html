Match Outcome Intelligence

ABOUT SCORELINE

ScoreLine delivers pre-match football predictions. For any fixture it estimates the chance of a home win, a draw or an away win, and it is open about how good those estimates really are.

Our mission

CLEAR, HONEST MATCH INSIGHT

We want anyone, whether a fan, a student or an analyst, to see what the data says about a match before kick-off, in plain numbers that are easy to read and easy to check.

ScoreLine predicts full-match results, not individual player stats. It covers the Premier League, La Liga and Ligue 1, using historical results in the widely used football-data.co.uk format.

Outcome probabilities

THREE NUMBERS, ALWAYS 100%

We evaluate thousands of past matches to learn how often each type of fixture ends in a home win, a draw or an away win. For a new match, every model splits 100% across those three outcomes, so the numbers are directly comparable and always add up.

A probability isn't a promise. If ScoreLine says 53% for a home win, that means in a large set of similar matches the home side wins a little over half the time, and doesn't in the rest.

Model inputs & training

WHAT GOES INTO A PREDICTION

Each model looks at the same kind of evidence, and only evidence that was available before kick-off. It never sees the result it's trying to predict.

Team strength (Elo)

Every team carries a rating that rises when it wins and falls when it loses. Beating a strong side earns more than beating a weak one. The bigger the gap between two teams, the more the stronger side is favoured, plus an allowance for home advantage.

Recent form

How many goals each team has been scoring and conceding in its last few games, with recent matches counting more than older ones, so current momentum shows up.

Expected scorelines (Poisson)

A goals model estimates how many goals each side should score against this particular opponent (for example 1.64 vs 1.19), then works out how likely every exact scoreline, and so every result, is.

Supervised learning

Three machine-learning models (logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost) learn from labelled past matches how much each input matters. We also combine models: an ensemble of Elo + Poisson, and an average of logistic regression and XGBoost.

You can switch between all of them in the predictor and compare their numbers side by side.

Validity & caveats

HOW GOOD IS IT, REALLY?

Better than guessing, close to the bookmakers, and far from perfect. Football is a low-scoring, high-randomness sport, and the honest numbers reflect that.

+10–12 pts

Accuracy gain over always backing the home side (about 52–54% vs 42%).

0.98 vs 1.08

Log loss (lower is better) of our best model vs just using historical result frequencies.

0.99 vs 0.98

Our best model vs the bookmakers on the 2025/26 test. Close, but the market is still ahead.

~1 in 4

Roughly how often top-flight matches end level, yet draws are almost never the single most likely outcome.

Football is unpredictable

One deflection or red card can flip a result. A 60% favourite still fails to win about 4 times in 10, so being "wrong" on a single match is expected.

Draws are hardest

In our model output, draw probabilities mostly sit between 15% and 33%, so the models rarely pick a draw outright even though roughly a quarter of top-flight matches end level.

What the models can't see

Injuries, suspensions, transfers, rotation, managerial changes and late team news are not inputs. Bookmakers do see these, which is part of why they stay ahead.

Keep in mind

ScoreLine predictions are provided for analytical insight and learning, not betting advice. Our models do not beat the bookmakers, and no model can remove the randomness of the sport.
