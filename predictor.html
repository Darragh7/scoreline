      Accuracy

      Log loss · lower is better

      Punishes a model for being confident and wrong. Saying 80% for a result that doesn't happen costs far more than saying 40%. Guessing ⅓ for everything scores about 1.10.

      Brier score · lower is better

      The average squared gap between the predicted probabilities and what actually happened (1 for the real result, 0 for the others). 0 would be perfect.

      RPS · lower is better

      Ranked Probability Score treats home → draw → away as an ordered scale, so predicting a draw when the home side wins is "less wrong" than predicting an away win.

      Accuracy · higher is better

      How often the most likely outcome was the actual result. Easy to read, but it ignores how confident the model was, so we rank models mainly by log loss.

      For insight, not betting advice

      ScoreLine's probabilities are estimates built from historical data. They don't know about injuries, suspensions, transfers or late team news, and even a 60% favourite fails to win 4 times in 10. Use them to explore and understand matches, not to place bets.
